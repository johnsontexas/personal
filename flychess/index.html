04

How it chooses a move

Step 1

Encode

For every legal move, the position it leads to is written onto 685 antennal-lobe projection neurons, the cells that carry smell into the mushroom body.

Step 2

Sparsify

The calyx keeps only the strongest 259 of the 5,177 Kenyon cells active, so each position gets its own sparse code.

Step 3

Vote

The output neurons (MBONs) sum that code. Approach cells push for the move, avoidance cells push against it. It plays the best-scoring move.

Step 4

Learn

Dopamine, a reward prediction error, rewrites the Kenyon cell to output synapses during play. A win produces a large burst.

Wiring, synapse counts and neuron positions are from the FlyWire connectome. Everything runs in your browser; only shared learning and the leaderboard touch a server.
